# New York private-sector hiring

Sources checked November 11, 2026. The guide uses the job’s work location. People can select one of the five boroughs, elsewhere in New York State, or an uncertain work location. All follow-ups use broad categories held in the browser tab.

## Rule map

| Answers or event | What the guide applies | What the applicant can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private company, 10 or more employees, prior conviction | Article 23-A restricts conviction-based denials to a direct relationship to the specific work or unreasonable risk to people or property. A mandatory legal bar may change coverage. [§ 750][size], [§ 751][scope], [§ 752][eligibility] | Ask which duties the employer is concerned about and compare them with the actual job description. |
| Employer considering a prior conviction | The employer considers the statutory factors, including age at the time of conduct, time since the conduct, job duties, seriousness, rehabilitation and good conduct. A qualifying certificate creates a presumption of rehabilitation for the offenses it covers. The city also applies these factors to prior convictions of applicants within its own coverage. [§ 753][factors], [city guidance][city] | Supply relevant examples if desired. If using a certificate, confirm its exact title and scope. |
| A conditional offer may be withdrawn outside confirmed NYC coverage | Statewide assessment can apply. Article 23-A does not itself set NYC’s notice-and-response period. [§ 752][eligibility], [§ 753][factors] | Ask whether the decision is open, which facts matter and when a response can be considered. |
| Denied employment, previous conviction, covered private employer | Section 754 gives a right to request written reasons. The employer’s 30 days run from the request. This is distinct from an applicant’s response or complaint deadline. [§ 754][reasons] | Send a dated request and keep the delivery record. |
| Under 10 or uncertain size, or an employer outside the ordinary private-sector scope | The state Article 23-A threshold and the NYC four-worker threshold are evaluated independently. Public agencies can be covered under different provisions. Role-specific legal bars, licensing and joint employment require individual review. [§ 750][size], [§ 751][scope], [NYC headcount guidance][citySize] | Confirm the workforce count, the hiring entity, and any specific legal requirement. |
| Question before an offer | For a covered job in NYC, conviction and pending-case inquiries generally wait until a conditional offer. Elsewhere or when location is uncertain, the guide flags local timing rules. [City guidance][city], [state Attorney General][ag] | Keep the wording and sequence of the question and offer. |
| Covered NYC job, conditional offer at risk because of a prior conviction or pending case | Employer discloses the criminal-history inquiry and its written analysis, holds the position open and allows a reasonable period of at least five business days from receipt of both. Missing or partial materials prompt a request. A final withdrawal following review must be communicated in writing within a reasonable time. [NYC rules][rules], [city guidance][city] | Check which materials arrived, note the dates, and send corrections or other information to the employer. |
| Pending case, favorable termination, youthful-offender outcome or possible sealing | NYC applies different factors to pending charges and earlier convictions. State Human Rights Law protects specified dispositions subject to statutory exceptions. Approximate age of a record does not establish sealing. [Executive Law § 296(15)–(16)][rights], [NY Courts][courts], [city guidance][city] | Verify the actual disposition or sealing order. The guide makes no determination about what must be disclosed. |
| Outside background-reporting company, possible error, or report-based rejection | Federal permissions, pre-decision documents, dispute rights and final notice are matched independently of state or city employer size. If an outside report contains conviction information covered by § 380-g(d), the requester supplies the applicant with Article 23-A. Internal searches do not trigger this third-party path by themselves. [FTC][ftc], [GBL § 380-g(d)][report] | Identify the entry, contact the reporting company and tell the employer. Keep separate track of any employer response window. |

## Reproduce a statewide result

Choose **An offer or job was affected**, **New York**, **Elsewhere in New York State**, **Private company**, **10–14 employees**, and **Retail or customer service**. Choose **Conviction**, **4–6 years** since the conduct and **1–3 years** since sentence completion. Select **After a rejection or withdrawn offer**. Leave optional answers blank and select **See my guide**. The result offers a dated request for written reasons under § 754 and explains the Article 23-A factors.

For a city example, choose **New York City**, **4–9 employees**, **After a conditional offer**, **The employer may withdraw my conditional offer because of my record**, and **Both the criminal-history inquiry and the written analysis**. The city response period appears. That size range alone does not establish the statewide § 754 threshold.

## Issues for review

- Local ordinances outside NYC, jobs spanning jurisdictions, and remote-work coverage.
- Public and federal employment, contractors, staffing or joint employment, licensed jobs, mandatory bars, and the scope of specific certificates or pardons.
- Exact dispositions, eligibility for sealing, and exceptions permitting particular employers to see records. Clean Slate implementation continues through the statutory implementation period.
- Additional New York consumer-report restrictions, salary-dependent reporting limits and occupational exceptions. No personal response date, filing deadline or prediction of hiring eligibility is calculated.
- Current agency intake options are available from the [New York State Division of Human Rights][dhr] and [NYC Commission on Human Rights][city].
