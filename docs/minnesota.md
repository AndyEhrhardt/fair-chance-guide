# Minnesota hiring guidance

Official sources checked November 11, 2026. The guide starts with ordinary private hiring in Minnesota and distinguishes public hiring where chapter 364 gives different rights. Applicant answers stay in browser memory. Documents mentioned in next steps are for the applicant to keep or share independently.

## How answers select a result

| Applicant’s answers | Guidance and limits | Practical step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private company, nonprofit or staffing agency, including a small employer or unknown headcount | The statewide timing rule generally applies without a minimum headcount. Where interviews are used, it turns on selection for an interview. If there is no interview, the relevant event is a conditional offer. [§ 364.021][timing] | Save the question, application, invitation and offer dates. |
| Asked before selection, during an initial interview given to everyone, or before an offer in a process without interviews | Each scenario gets a timing explanation. Selection can precede the interview itself. An initial screen for everyone can function as an application. A later offer does not establish when the original question was asked. [MDHR examples][technical] | Retain the wording. A private-employer inquiry can be reported to the [MDHR Ban the Box intake][intake]. |
| Question after interview selection or after a conditional offer when no interviews were used | The ordinary timing threshold has generally been reached. An advance statement of a disqualification for a particular position is distinguishable from a demand for personal history. [§ 364.021(a), (c)][timing] | Ask which record and duties are at issue. Save a posting that appears to require an answer. |
| Statutory duty claimed, unclear employer type, or specialized role | A statutory duty to check or consider criminal history can change the timing rule. A company policy, industry, or client’s requirement alone cannot establish an exemption. The Department of Corrections and certain jobs or processes have explicit exceptions. [§ 364.021(b)][timing], [§ 364.09][exceptions] | Identify the hiring entity, legal provision and actual duties before deciding that an exception applies. |
| Private job, offer at risk or final rejection involving a record | The timing law does not itself create a private-employer response window. MDHR advises employers to consider job duties, seriousness and elapsed time. [MDHR hiring guidance][mdhr] | Ask which entry caused concern, whether the decision is final and when corrections can be considered. |
| Minnesota state or local public job involving a prior conviction | For covered public positions, direct relationship, rehabilitation and present fitness matter. A final denial based at least partly on a conviction requires written reasons and grievance and reapplication information. Certain records are restricted in public hiring. These rules have occupational exceptions. [§ 364.03][public-review], [§ 364.04][public-records], [§ 364.05][public-denial], [§ 364.09][exceptions] | Request the written decision from the public hiring authority. Use the [public grievance route][enforcement]. |
| Outside consumer report for a job applied for | Minnesota requires advance written disclosure with a free-copy checkbox. If requested, the reporting company must send a free copy within 24 hours of giving its report to the employer. There is no obligation to send a nonexistent report. Unsolicited positions and specified current-employee investigations are exceptions. [§ 13C.02][copy] | Return the copy request and ask when the employer received the report. |
| Outside criminal report, possible error | A fee-based business screening service investigates a nonfrivolous dispute without charge and gives notice of the outcome within 30 days of receipt. A service with knowledge of a sealed, expunged or pardoned record must promptly delete it. The statute recognizes compliance with the applicable federal FCRA provisions. [§ 332.70, subds. 1, 3, 3a, 5][screening] | Identify the incorrect entry, send supporting copies to the screening service and notify the employer. |
| Final employment decision confirmed to rely at least partly on the outside report | The employer gives notice of the report’s use and the reporting agency’s contact and dispute information. If the applicant did not already receive a copy, the notice explains that right. The agency must provide a free copy within five days after receiving the applicant’s written request. [§ 13C.03][adverse] | Send the request to the reporting agency and retain proof of delivery. A rejection alone cannot establish this trigger. |
| Outside report regardless of the ordinary state timing rule | Existing federal disclosure, permission, pre-decision, final-notice and dispute guidance is matched separately. Internal searches or an unidentified provider cannot establish third-party report rights. Special federal transportation procedures may require review. [FTC][ftc], [15 USC § 1681b][fcra] | Identify the source and which entry the employer relied on before choosing a dispute procedure. |

## Try the interview example

1. Choose **I was asked about my record**.
2. Choose **Minnesota**. Leave city or county blank.
3. Choose **Private company**, **1–4 employees**, and **Retail or customer service**.
4. Choose **Conviction**.
5. Choose **4–6 years** since the conduct and **1–3 years** since sentence completion.
6. Choose **Application or early interview**. For the timing follow-up, choose **I was asked before being selected for an interview**. Leave other optional answers blank and select **See my guide**.

The result explains interview selection and links to MDHR’s private-employer reporting form. Change only that follow-up to **I was asked after being selected for an interview** to see why the timing result changes. For hiring without interviews, compare the two conditional-offer choices.

## Other test cases

- **Conditional offer at risk.** Select a private employer, **An offer or job was affected**, **After a conditional offer**, and the answer that the employer is considering withdrawing the offer because of the record. The result suggests a response and makes no personal deadline calculation.
- **Possible report error.** Select **A background check is happening**, **During a background check**, **An outside background-reporting company**, and **Yes** to a suspected error. The dispute card identifies the reporting company as the recipient. If a free copy was requested but has not arrived, the separate 24-hour rule is explained.
- **Uncertain coverage.** Select an employer that identified a statutory check or choose **Not sure** about that requirement. The result asks which law covers the job. Rights involving a commercial report are evaluated independently for an identified Minnesota employer.

## Open questions

City and county ordinances, the exact legal mandate for regulated positions, licensing-board procedures, individual expungement orders, employee investigations, and cross-jurisdiction or federal coverage require separate review. Neither the job category nor the age of a record establishes a disqualification or entitlement to sealing. The app supplies general information and does not determine an individual deadline or submit complaints.
